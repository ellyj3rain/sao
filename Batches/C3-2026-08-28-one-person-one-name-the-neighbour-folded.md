an ID card for Titus Conno when he died. The walk found one symptom over
three leaks, and the neighbour framework read end to end (its installed
tree, file and line - mechanics studied, no files lifted).

**The leaks.** (1) `spawnShellNamed` stamped Identity's placeholder
"Unnamed"/"Survivor" over the engine's freshly generated name one call
before `backfillName` existed to read it back - every native record was
"Unnamed Survivor" for the project's whole life, the menu falling back to
raw record ids, every journal titled "Unnamed's journal", every
survivor-to-survivor belief keyed to the same string. (2) The neighbour
names its people from its own profile table and never writes the engine
descriptor, so the descriptor carries a random engine name the neighbour
never shows - and adoption read the DESCRIPTOR. His menu and his ID card
said the profile name; our menu said the descriptor's. Two names, one
body: exactly Randy/Grady versus Titus Conno. (3) The [B45] hold on his
prompt channels tested the global `KS`; his global is `KnoxSurvivors` and
`KS` is a per-file local in his tree - the hold had never engaged once,
which is how his narration kept reaching the operator's screen after the
record said it was held.

**One name (DR-014).** Placeholders never overwrite: the engine's
generated name now survives spawn, the backfill reads it into the record,
and existing "Unnamed" records take a real name at their next
materialization (their stale belief keys age out on the ordinary horizons;

retitled where the placeholder era misnamed them, and an ID card - the
neighbour's own item types, proven on this install by the card the
operator looted - ensured in the pocket, named from `Identity.knownName`,
the same renderer the menu reads. Death drops what the living carried:
death uses the living name. For the neighbour's people the profile name is
the name: adoption prefers it, renames a record that predates the read,

which lands after his file-scope registration by construction; strips are
